---

## THE FLOW CYCLE (4 Phases — Never Skip One)

```
STRUGGLE → RELEASE → FLOW → RECOVERY
  (load)    (let go)  (peak)  (rebuild)
```

1. **STRUGGLE** (20-45 min): Brain overloads with information. Feels hard. This is REQUIRED — stress hormones (norepinephrine, cortisol) prepare the neurochemical shift. DO NOT QUIT during struggle.
2. **RELEASE** (variable): Take your mind OFF the problem. Walk, breathe, do something low-cognitive. The prefrontal cortex downregulates (transient hypofrontality begins).
3. **FLOW** (45-90 min): Full absorption. Dopamine, norepinephrine, anandamide, serotonin, endorphins flood the brain simultaneously. Time distortion, ego dissolution, pattern recognition spike.
4. **RECOVERY** (mandatory): The neurochemicals deplete. You MUST actively recover or you cannot re-enter flow. This is where most people fail — they push through and destroy tomorrow's capacity.

**Integration with 910-Day OS:** Struggle = Singer (notice the resistance, don't run). Release = Singer (surrender). Flow = Naval (leverage, compounding). Recovery = respect the cycle.

## 10 NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

### 1. Flow Before Phone
Your phone does NOT get touched until after Flow Block 1 is complete. Every notification hijacks your dopamine system and makes flow entry 4x harder. Phone stays in another room, airplane mode, until 08:30.

### 2. The 90-Second Rule (Wake Up and Flow)
From eyes open to working: 90 seconds. No coffee ritual, no journaling, no biohacks first. Sit down and START. The brain is naturally in a theta-alpha transitional state upon waking — this is the easiest flow entry point of the entire day. Every minute you delay, the window closes.

### 3. The Desktop Rule
Work happens on a desktop/laptop. Never on a phone or tablet. The physical posture, screen size, and separation from notifications create an environmental flow trigger.

### 4. Inverted Morning Routine
Traditional morning routines (meditation, journaling, exercise, cold plunge) are done AFTER Flow Block 1, not before. These are recovery activities — doing them first wastes your peak neurochemical window.

### 5. Work Compression
Parkinson's Law: work expands to fill the time. Set aggressive deadlines for each flow block. A 3-hour block with a hard stop at the end produces more than a vague "I'll work until I'm done" approach. Time pressure is a flow trigger.

### 6. Boring Breaks (Active Recovery Only)
Between flow blocks: NO social media, NO news, NO Netflix, NO scrolling. These hijack dopamine and prevent re-entry into flow. Recovery must be BORING to the dopamine system but ACTIVE for the body. See the recovery protocol below.

### 7. Six Alarms / Temporal Landmarks
Set 6 alarms throughout the day as phase transition signals. These create temporal landmarks that prevent time blindness and maintain the structure. Each alarm = a decision point, not a suggestion.

### 8. The Domino Habit
Identify the ONE tiny action that starts your flow block (opening the specific file, writing one sentence, running one test). This is your domino — once it falls, the rest follows. Make it stupidly small. Reduce friction to zero.

### 9. Challenge-Skills Balance (+4%)
Every flow block must be slightly beyond your current skill level — approximately 4% harder. Too easy = boredom. Too hard = anxiety. Both block flow. Before each block, calibrate: "Is this stretching me just enough?"

### 10. Subconscious Recruitment
Before recovery periods, write your hardest problem as a QUESTION (not a statement). "How do I structure the API to handle X?" Then do something completely unrelated. Your subconscious processes it during release phase. Solutions appear during or after recovery.

## ACTIVE RECOVERY PROTOCOL

Recovery is NOT optional. It is phase 4 of the cycle. Skip it and you lose tomorrow.

**DO (Active Recovery):**
- Cold exposure (ice bath, cold shower — 2-5 min)
- Sauna or contrast therapy
- Walk in nature, especially near water
- HIIT or strength training (20-30 min)
- Power nap (20 min max, before 2pm)
- Breathwork (box breathing, Wim Hof)
- Stretching, yoga
- Cooking a meal (tactile, low-cognitive)

**DO NOT (Passive Consumption — Destroys Recovery):**
- Social media scrolling
- News consumption
- Netflix/YouTube rabbit holes
- Texting conversations
- Email checking between blocks
- Any screen-based dopamine hit

## FLOW BLOCKERS (The Three Enemies)

1. **Overwhelm**: Too many options, unclear priorities. Fix: Pick ONE deliverable per block. Write it down. Ignore everything else.
2. **Distraction**: External (notifications, people) or internal (anxiety, rumination). Fix: Environment design. Phone gone. Door closed. Noise-canceling headphones. Internal: 2-minute meditation to clear, then start.
3. **Friction**: Setup time, unclear next steps, missing tools. Fix: End each flow block by writing the FIRST action for tomorrow's block. Reduce activation energy to zero.

**Sleep**: 7-8 hours is non-negotiable. Sleep debt is the #1 flow killer. One bad night reduces flow probability by 50%+.
